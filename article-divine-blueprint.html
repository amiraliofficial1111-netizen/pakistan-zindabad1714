Did God Have a Constitution Before America? The Divine Blueprint That Could Change Everything

What if a constitution existed before America, and creation itself was ordered by faith, love, and obedience?

Mark H Steffen · 16 October 2026 · 4 minute read

When we hear the word constitution, we usually think of governments, laws, and the principles that protect a nation's freedom. The United States Constitution, for example, was created to establish a system of government, define responsibilities, and safeguard the rights of its people.

But what if the idea of a constitution existed long before America was founded? What if the universe itself was created according to a set of principles designed to guide humanity toward a greater purpose?

In his thought-provoking book, The Constitution of God, author Mark H. Steffen explores this fascinating possibility, presenting a perspective that connects divine creation, human responsibility, spiritual freedom, and the founding principles of America.

A Divine Blueprint Before Human Civilization

Every successful structure begins with a plan. A house needs a foundation, a nation needs laws, and a community needs shared values. Without these guiding principles, even the strongest institutions can eventually fall apart.

Steffen applies this same reasoning to the creation of the universe.

According to his interpretation, God established an original set of principles long before human civilization existed. These principles formed what he describes as a cosmic constitution, a divine framework governing the relationship between God, creation, and the spiritual world.

At the heart of this framework were three essential principles: absolute faith, absolute love, and absolute obedience.

These were not simply religious ideals. In Steffen's account, they represented the foundation upon which God's original vision for creation was built.

The ultimate goal was not merely to create a world where human beings could survive. It was to establish a Kingdom of Heaven on Earth, where humanity could live in harmony with God and fulfill its spiritual potential.

Adam and Eve: Humanity's First Constitutional Responsibility

The story of Adam and Eve is familiar to millions of people. However, Steffen encourages readers to look beyond the traditional understanding of their actions in the Garden of Eden.

In his interpretation, Adam and Eve were chosen to begin the establishment of God's heavenly kingdom in the physical world.

Their responsibility was to mature spiritually, develop a relationship with God, and eventually become the foundation of a God-centered human family.

Genesis 1:28, which speaks of being fruitful, multiplying, and exercising dominion over creation, becomes especially significant in this discussion.

Steffen views this instruction as an early expression of God's constitutional principles.

Yet humanity was also given free will.

That freedom created an extraordinary opportunity, but it also introduced the possibility of choosing a different path.

The failure of Adam and Eve, influenced by Lucifer, represents a turning point in Steffen's account. Instead of fulfilling the original divine plan, humanity became separated from the spiritual relationship God intended.

This raises an uncomfortable but important question: What happens when freedom is exercised without responsibility?

The Surprising Connection Between God's Plan and America's Constitution

One of the most intriguing aspects of Steffen's work is his comparison between divine principles and the founding ideals of the United States.

At first glance, the creation of heaven and the establishment of an American government might seem like entirely unrelated subjects.

However, Steffen identifies similarities in their underlying purposes.

Both, in his interpretation, depend upon clearly established principles, individual responsibility, and the proper exercise of freedom.

America's Constitution was designed to provide a framework within which a nation could develop while protecting liberty and preventing the misuse of power.

Similarly, Steffen argues that God's original constitution was intended to guide human development while preserving the freedom necessary for spiritual growth.

Neither system eliminates the responsibility of those who live within it.

A constitution provides direction, but its success ultimately depends upon the people entrusted with following its principles.

Are We Losing Sight of Our Original Purpose?

Modern society has made remarkable advances in science, technology, communication, and individual opportunity. Yet questions about morality, purpose, and human responsibility remain as relevant as ever.

Why do societies struggle despite having laws and institutions? Why does freedom sometimes lead to conflict rather than cooperation? Can humanity create lasting peace without a shared moral foundation?

These questions are central to the broader discussion Steffen presents.

His argument invites readers to consider whether humanity's greatest challenges arise not simply from failures of government, but from a deeper separation from the principles that were meant to guide human existence.

Whether readers agree with his interpretation or approach it with skepticism, the comparison encourages reflection on the relationship between spiritual values and civic responsibility.

A Question Worth Exploring

Perhaps the most compelling idea behind The Constitution of God is that humanity's future may depend on understanding its original purpose.

For Mark H. Steffen, the concept of a divine constitution offers more than an explanation of the past. It provides a framework for examining the choices humanity makes today and the kind of world future generations might inherit.

The question is no longer simply whether God had a constitution before America.

It is whether understanding that original blueprint could change the way we think about freedom, responsibility, faith, and the future of humanity.
